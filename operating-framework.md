# The Munger–Buffett Operating Framework

This is a practical decision system derived from the study. It is not a substitute for domain expertise. It is a way to apply expertise more reliably.

## The master sequence

### 1. Define the actual decision

- What must be decided, by whom, and by when?
- What outcome are we optimizing?
- What is the default if we do nothing?
- Is this reversible, costly to reverse, or irreversible?
- Which facts are known, estimated, and assumed?

Write the decision in one sentence. If it cannot be stated clearly, analysis has begun too early.

### 2. Establish survival constraints

- What can cause permanent loss, forced sale, insolvency, reputational destruction, or unrecoverable harm?
- Which obligations persist if revenue, markets, or personal circumstances deteriorate?
- Are leverage, liquidity mismatch, counterparty dependence, or correlated risks hiding in the structure?
- What margin of safety is required because the estimate may be wrong?

Reject decisions that offer attractive expected value but an unacceptable chance of ending the game.

### 3. Test the circle of competence

- Can I explain the causal engine without jargon?
- Which two or three variables determine most of the outcome?
- How has this system failed historically?
- What would an experienced operator know that I do not?
- Can I distinguish a hard problem from a merely unfamiliar one?

Allowed outputs are **inside**, **learnable before the deadline**, and **too hard**. “Interesting” is not a competence category.

### 4. Build the model lattice

Use only models that can change the decision:

- Economics: incentives, scarcity, competitive advantage, opportunity cost, supply and demand.
- Accounting: cash generation, capital needs, hidden liabilities, normalization, ownership dilution.
- Probability: base rates, expected value, distributions, dependence, fat tails.
- Psychology: social proof, authority, confirmation, denial, consistency, envy, deprivation, availability.
- Engineering: bottlenecks, redundancy, failure modes, safety factors, feedback loops.
- Biology and systems: adaptation, selection, equilibrium, nonlinear interaction.
- History: comparable episodes, institutional path dependence, cycles.

State what each relevant model predicts. Do not use model names as decoration.

### 5. Audit incentives and psychology separately

**Incentives**

- Who benefits from each outcome?
- What is rewarded, punished, measured, or ignored?
- Does the adviser gain if I act, wait, or remain confused?
- What behavior will this system produce after repeated use?

**Psychology**

- Which conclusion do I want to be true?
- What commitment, identity, authority, or crowd am I defending?
- Am I reacting to loss, envy, scarcity, stress, or recent vivid evidence?
- Which tendencies reinforce one another into a lollapalooza effect?

Do not treat sincerity as proof that incentives are irrelevant.

### 6. Invert and pre-mortem

Assume the decision failed badly. List the causes.

- What was obvious in retrospect?
- Which dependency broke?
- What did we fail to measure?
- Where did leverage or correlation appear?
- Which small ethical or reporting compromise grew?
- What did success itself make fragile?

Then remove or monitor the most consequential paths. Prevention usually has higher certainty than optimization.

### 7. Force disconfirmation

- Write the strongest opposing thesis as its best advocate would.
- Identify three facts that would falsify the preferred view.
- Search specifically for those facts.
- Ask what a smart, informed critic sees differently.
- Separate “I disagree” from “this evidence would change my mind.”

No decision is ready while its strongest objection exists only as a caricature.

### 8. Compare opportunity costs

Compare the proposal with:

- the best available alternative;
- waiting for more information;
- a smaller or reversible experiment;
- deploying the same money or attention elsewhere;
- preserving cash, time, or option value.

Sunk cost is excluded. The hurdle is prospective value, not how much has already been invested.

### 9. Decide, size, and specify conditions

- What is the decision and why?
- How large should the commitment be given probability, downside, liquidity, and confidence?
- What must be true for it to work?
- What evidence triggers expansion, reduction, or exit?
- Who owns the next action?
- When will the decision be reviewed?

Concentration is the result of earned conviction plus survival capacity. It is not a personality trait.

### 10. Record and learn

Before the outcome is known, record expectations, assumptions, probabilities, alternatives, and emotional state. At review:

- Separate decision quality from outcome quality.
- Identify skill, luck, missing knowledge, and broken process.
- Update the relevant checklist or model.
- Track repeated omissions and repeated rationalizations.
- Preserve what was known at the time; do not rewrite the original thesis.

## Investment variant

### Business

- Explain how the company makes money and why customers choose it.
- Estimate normalized owner earnings and required reinvestment.
- Determine whether incremental capital earns attractive returns.
- Identify the moat, the evidence it is widening or narrowing, and the mechanism of durability.
- Distinguish secular growth from favorable cycle conditions.

### People and structure

- Map compensation, capital-allocation incentives, dilution, related-party exposure, and reporting choices.
- Ask whether management communicates like an owner when results are bad.
- Test whether the organization can function without a singular founder.

### Price and risk

- Use a range of values, not a precision point.
- Model adverse conditions, financing needs, and correlated exposures.
- Define permanent impairment separately from price volatility.
- Compare expected return with the best available alternative and cash.

### Decision discipline

- State why the market may be wrong.
- State why you may be wrong.
- Size for knowledge and downside, not excitement.
- Establish thesis-breakers; do not convert every disappointment into “long term.”

## Product variant

- Define the consequential user decision or job; do not begin with features.
- Identify the bottleneck in the user's current system.
- Measure whether the product improves a decision, reduces an error, or saves meaningful time.
- Incentivize maintained truth: sources, changed evidence, uncertainty, and correction.
- Run a pre-mortem for hallucination, stale context, privacy, permissioning, automation error, and false confidence.
- Prefer a narrow, high-value workflow with accumulated proprietary context over generic AI surface area.
- Preserve a “too hard / insufficient evidence” state. A system forced to answer every query will manufacture certainty.

For each dossier, keep:

```text
Current claim
Confidence and evidence quality
Attached sources
What changed
What strengthened
What weakened
Open questions
Decision / next move
Review trigger
```

## Hiring and partnership variant

- Test reliability before brilliance: Does the person do what they said, especially on small commitments?
- Examine behavior under conflicting incentives and stress.
- Choose people you would be comfortable becoming more like.
- Define the outcome and grant autonomy proportionate to judgment and integrity.
- Keep irreversible authority narrow until deserved trust is established.
- Design compensation around durable outcomes, including the costs created for others.
- Never use cultural language to conceal unmeasured performance or tolerated misconduct.

## Personal spending variant

Classify the purchase:

- Financial capital: expected cash return or preserved downside.
- Capability capital: health, skill, tools, energy, or time that improves future capacity.
- Relationship capital: shared time and trust that cannot always be deferred.
- Consumption: enjoyment with no claimed instrumental return.

Then ask:

- What is the realistic return, including time and attention?
- Is the benefit durable, recurring, or quickly habituated?
- Can I test it at smaller scale?
- Does it create a fixed obligation or reduce resilience?
- Which future experience becomes unavailable if I always defer this category?

Consumption is not automatically irrational. Calling consumption an “investment” to avoid honest tradeoffs is.

## AI assistants and automation variant

Before automating:

- What error or delay is being removed?
- What happens when the automation is wrong?
- Can it make an external commitment, spend money, publish, delete, or expose private information?
- Are sources and transformations auditable?
- Is stale context visibly dated?
- Does the output contain fact, inference, and recommendation as separate layers?
- Which action requires human approval?

Automate reversible collection and maintenance aggressively. Gate consequential external actions. Optimize for fewer high-quality decisions, not more notifications.

## Weekly practice

Once a week:

1. Review one consequential decision before knowing its result.
2. Find one belief you have not seriously tried to disprove.
3. Identify one incentive producing unwanted behavior.
4. Move one recurring problem from exhortation into system design.
5. Add one model only if it changes how a real decision is made.
6. Remove one commitment that increases ruin or distraction risk.
7. Name the best opportunity currently being rejected and why.

The purpose is not to sound more like Munger or Buffett. It is to make fewer preventable mistakes and preserve the ability to act when a rare opportunity deserves it.
