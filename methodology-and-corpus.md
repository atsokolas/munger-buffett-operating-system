# Methodology and Corpus Audit

## Research objective

Build a source-first, chronological understanding of Charlie Munger and Warren Buffett from their own words, with primary emphasis on Munger, then convert it into a usable framework for judgment, investing, work, institutional design, and learning.

The study sought repeated principles, changes over time, boundary conditions, and disconfirming examples. It did not optimize for famous quotations or attempt to validate a preselected heroic narrative.

## Completion definition

This study is complete as a bounded synthesis, not as a claim to have collected every public utterance by either person.

Completion means:

- the major primary-source chronologies are represented across their working lives;
- the important ideas are triangulated across more than one source family where possible;
- major changes, tensions, and errors are included;
- claims in the final deliverables are calibrated to source quality;
- the four promised outputs are present and usable.

The source cutoff is August 3, 2026. New archives, better transcripts, and later-discovered documents may improve the corpus without invalidating completion.

## Deliverables

- `executive-brief.md` — concise conclusion and implications.
- `cited-reference.md` — chronological and thematic synthesis with source links and caveats.
- `operating-framework.md` — decision procedures and applied templates.
- `methodology-and-corpus.md` — this audit.

The public repository contains the four deliverables plus the core-framework notes under `notes/`. The underlying corpus of primary sources is not distributed here; see `corpus-sources.md` for how to rebuild it from public archives.

## Source hierarchy

### Grade A — official primary

Issuer-, institution-, university-, regulator-, or original-publisher-hosted text or recording. Examples include Berkshire shareholder letters, Berkshire/Wesco archives, CNBC's Warren Buffett Archive, Munger's 2014 Berkshire retrospective, and official institutional publications.

Use: thematic claims and exact quotations after checking the underlying text.

### Grade B — faithful primary mirror

Scans or transcripts of original material hosted by a reputable archive or school, including the Ivey Buffett Partnership compilation and clean archival copies of Munger speeches and essays.

Use: thematic claims; exact quotations when extraction quality is clean and text has been checked.

### Grade C — third-party transcript or detailed notes

Contemporary meeting reconstructions, edited transcripts, or archived attendee notes, including much of the Wesco annual-meeting and Daily Journal material.

Use: chronology, theme triangulation, examples, and paraphrase. Exact quotations require independent confirmation.

### Grade D — discovery or weak reconstruction

Compressed summaries, noisy OCR, quote compilations, or unverified secondary retellings.

Use: discovery only unless corroborated. No load-bearing quotation.

## Research procedure

1. Establish official archive anchors and a dated local corpus manifest.
2. Extract text, adding OCR sidecars where source PDFs were image-based.
3. Read chronologically in bounded tranches rather than searching only for known phrases.
4. Record principles, new mechanisms, changes from earlier views, and tensions.
5. Maintain a cross-source core framework for Munger.
6. Compare the Munger chronology with Buffett's letters and live meeting answers.
7. Prefer paraphrase when source quality cannot support exact quotation.
8. Build final arguments only after the chronology was substantially closed.

## Citation method

The final documents use source names, dates, and official links where available. The core-framework notes retain finer-grained anchors to source lines or years.

Quotation standard:

- Grade A or clean Grade B source required for a load-bearing exact quote.
- OCR text must be checked against the scan when wording matters.
- Grade C notes are paraphrased unless independently confirmed.
- Allegory, parody, draft writing, and retrospective reconstruction are labeled as such.
- Inference is distinguished from a claim made directly by Munger or Buffett.

## Material provenance limitations

### Berkshire 1970–1976

The official Berkshire letter archive begins its separately posted annual letters in 1977. This study closed the earlier transition period using a third-party compiled text-bearing PDF and yearly image-heavy annual-report backups. The evidence supports the chronology and themes but not fragile exact quotation without a future OCR and scan comparison.

### Wesco shareholder letters

Some letter batches were read from compiled scans or archival mirrors rather than freshly downloaded official text. OCR sidecars were used where direct extraction failed. The letters are strong primary material, but exact wording from noisy pages needs scan verification.

### Wesco annual meetings, 1999–2011

Most surviving local sources are attendee notes or third-party reconstructions, often explicitly described as non-verbatim and sometimes reorganized by subject. They are valuable for repeated themes, examples, and evolution across years. They are weaker evidence for precise wording.

### Daily Journal annual meetings, 2013–2023

The early 2013–2015 records are compressed third-party notes; the 2015 notes explicitly disclaim transcript status. Later years are fuller transcript mirrors but are not generally issuer-hosted. The study uses them for late-life triangulation and paraphrases load-bearing material.

### Munger archival essays and speeches

The archive includes a mixture: direct letters and essays, edited or mirrored speech transcripts, a 1984 draft opinion piece, OCR-compressed congressional testimony, and a deliberately exaggerated 2011 parody. Each was interpreted according to genre and provenance. The parody is not treated as neutral crisis history.

### Official Berkshire annual-meeting sampling

The official CNBC transcript sample covers 1994–2003 rather than every meeting year. These ten years were selected to test live doctrine across the mid-1990s, technology bubble, 9/11, Enron-era accounting, and the General Re derivative transition. The letters provide the continuous annual backbone through 2009. Extending official meeting coverage to 2004 onward is a useful future enhancement, not a conceptual prerequisite for this synthesis.

## Manifest discrepancies

The private corpus manifest was refreshed at completion to point five important records to their usable OCR sidecars and to add the compiled 1969–1976 Berkshire source plus seven image-only annual-report backups. It remains a research inventory rather than a fully normalized scholarly catalog. Remaining limitations include:

- several individual Wesco mirror records still show image-only extraction, while the same years are usable through the compiled OCR record;
- official CNBC transcript pages used in close reading are not all locally mirrored;
- file availability does not imply equal provenance or quotation fitness.

Accordingly, the manifest should be used with the core-framework notes and this audit, not alone. These bookkeeping limitations do not create a missing conceptual source tranche, but they prevent claiming a perfectly normalized scholarly corpus.

## Anti-hagiography controls

The synthesis deliberately retained evidence that complicates the framework:

- Berkshire textiles: cheapness could not overcome poor incremental economics.
- Buffalo Evening News: eventual strategic success carried substantial interim losses and opportunity cost.
- Dexter Shoe: business error compounded by payment in Berkshire shares.
- General Re: acquisition inherited major derivative and cultural problems.
- ConocoPhillips: a large commodity purchase at unfavorable timing.
- GEICO credit card: a failed adjacency that Berkshire stopped.
- Wal-Mart: acknowledged omission.
- Railroads: a rational update, but in Munger's words “too late smart.”
- CORT: acknowledged timing and stubbornness problems.
- BYD, China, and Wells Fargo: areas where admiration or confidence can be tested against the same incentive and governance standards.
- Alibaba: a significant late-life investment error.
- Select derivatives and insurance float: exceptions that complicate simplistic anti-derivative and anti-leverage readings.
- Reduced expectations at scale: historical returns are not a timeless prospective promise.

These examples are not appended as ritual balance. They define where the method fails, where conditions matter, and which popular slogans are unsafe.

## What was not attempted

- A complete biography or reconstruction of private deliberations.
- Exhaustive coverage of every annual meeting, television appearance, or secondary interview.
- Independent forensic recalculation of every Berkshire valuation or investment return.
- A comprehensive history of Berkshire subsidiaries.
- Treating Munger and Buffett as identical thinkers.
- Presenting their public-policy views as established facts merely because their business record was exceptional.

## Reproducibility and future updates

A future researcher can extend the work by:

1. mirroring and indexing official Berkshire meeting transcripts after 2003;
2. OCR-checking the 1970–1976 annual reports against the compiled text;
3. refreshing the manifest to recognize OCR sidecars and newer files;
4. replacing third-party Daily Journal or Wesco notes if official recordings or transcripts become available;
5. checking exact quotations against scans before publication;
6. appending a dated change log rather than silently rewriting source judgments.

Any update should state whether it changes a conclusion, raises or lowers confidence, or merely improves provenance.

## Final audit judgment

The corpus is broad and chronological enough to support the final thesis. The continuous backbone—Buffett's Partnership and Berkshire letters, Munger's letters and major speeches, and representative official meeting transcripts—is strong. Meeting-note provenance and manifest normalization remain imperfect, so the study should not be represented as a definitive scholarly edition.

It is, however, complete for its stated purpose: a source-grounded account of the Munger–Buffett system, its evolution, its limits, and its practical application.
